# Travel Through the City

## The Main Downtown Streets

### _Vicus_ (The Everyday Avenue)

- The Look: A bustling, stone-paved street lined with multi-story apartment buildings and open-air shops.
- The Features: The road was paved with large, flat polygonal (many-sided) basalt stones fitted together like puzzle pieces. Deep ruts were worn right into the stone from heavy iron-rimmed wagon wheels.
- The Activity: Shopkeepers set up counters right at the edge of the street, spilling out into the road. To cross a _vicus_, you had to jump across large stepping stones raised above the street. These stones allowed you to cross without getting your sandals wet from the water, animal waste, and trash that washed down the street when it rained. Wagons were designed with high axles so they could roll right over these stones. [1, 2]

### _Clivus_ (The Hilly Street)

- The Look: A steep, climbing road winding up a hillside, often leading to grand temples or palaces. [3]
- The Features: Because it was on a slope, the paving stones were often grooved or textured to give draft animals (like mules and oxen) traction so they wouldn't slip.
- The Activity: These streets featured heavy stone drainage gutters on both sides. When it rained, water roared down the _clivus_ like a river, washing dirt and debris away into the main valleys. Walking up a _clivus_ was exhausting, and you would constantly have to dodge heavy carts struggling to brake as they rolled down the incline.

### _Via_ (The Grand Downtown Highway)

- The Look: The widest, grandest street in the city, like Rome's _Via Sacra_. It felt more like a monumental plaza than a road.
- The Features: It was lined with towering marble columns, triumphal arches, and massive stone temples. Beneath this grand street lay the city's engineering marvels: a massive network of brick-lined underground sewers (like the _Cloaca Maxima_ in Rome). [4]
- The Activity: Runoff from public baths and street-side fountains constantly flushed through the gutters and down into the sewer grates below, keeping this main highway remarkably clean. It was built wide enough for military parades, religious processions, and massive crowds of citizens.

## Small Streets and Sidewalks

### _Angiportus_ (The Dark Alley)

- The Look: A cramped, dark, and claustrophobic passage sandwiched between towering apartment buildings (_insulae_).
- The Features: Most _angiportus_ lanes were not paved at all; they were just dirt or packed gravel. Because the apartment buildings were so high and built so close together, the roofs almost touched, blocking out the sunlight and making the alley pitch black even during the day.
- The Activity: There were no stepping stones or sewers here. Rainwater pooled into thick, smelly mud mixed with garbage thrown out of upper-floor windows. It was an excellent place for thieves to hide, and average travelers avoided them at night. [5, 6]

### _Semita_ (The Footpath or Sidewalk)

- The Look: A raised stone walkway running parallel to a busy _vicus_ or _via_.
- The Features: The _semita_ was raised anywhere from several inches to a few feet higher than the actual roadway, separated by a heavy stone curb.
- The Activity: This was the pedestrian safe zone. It kept you safely elevated away from the dangerous, heavy wagon traffic and the dirty water flowing through the street gutters.

### _Ambitus_ (The Maintenance Gap)

- The Look: A tiny, secret-looking slit between the walls of two adjacent buildings, usually only two to three feet wide.
- The Features: Law required this gap to prevent fires from easily spreading from one building to the next and to allow building repairs. It was unpaved dirt.
- The Activity: No one used this for casual travel. It was often dark, damp, and choked with weeds or trash, used only by builders or slaves performing maintenance on the outer walls of the buildings.


## Visual Look
Here is a visual look at what these different roads and engineering features looked like, using both modern photographs of surviving ruins and artistic 3D models of how they looked in their prime.

### 1. The Everyday Avenue (_Vicus_) and Stepping Stones

This is the clearest example of what an everyday commercial street looked like. The photos from the preserved ruins of [[Pompeii](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11248/the-streets-of-pompeii/)](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11248/the-streets-of-pompeii/) show the massive, flat basalt paving stones and the iconic raised crosswalk blocks. You can clearly see how the blocks were spaced out so that pedestrian feet stayed dry, while the wheels of passing wagons could roll perfectly through the gaps.
![[Streets_roads_ways.png]]

### 2. The Grand Highway (_Via_)

Artistic 3D reconstructions of the famous _[[Via Sacra](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/066m8b)](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/066m8b)_ (The Sacred Way) show how different a grand central highway looked compared to a regular neighborhood street. Instead of tight apartment blocks, it was lined with massive marble columns, temples, and grand public squares where crowds could gather for military triumphs and parades.
![[Streets_roads_ways-1.png]]
### 3. Underground Sewers and Drain Grates

Beneath those grand city streets lay the sewers. While some water drained through stone slats in the street curb, major crossroads featured stone or iron manhole covers and grates. The water poured down into massive brick-and-stone tunnels like the famous _[[Cloaca Maxima](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/03hp29)](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/03hp29)_ (The Great Sewer) beneath Rome, which still carries water today.
